# Sample design: the fresh-word run

Status: decided on November 10, 2026 unless marked **open**. No experiments have been run. Prepared with Claude Fable 5.2, which takes over as assistant from this document onward.

Implementation update: the [collector review protocol](implementation-protocol.md) supersedes the former automatic settings fallbacks and post-collection scorer-freeze sequence. Model lineup, stimuli, prompts, repetitions and the $20/provider limits remain as decided. No new study responses were collected during the repair.

## What changed and why

The original fabricated words are public. The historical repository holds roughly 5,000 raw responses, so each original word sits beside hundreds of example answers, both refusals and inventions. Any current model may have seen them. Whether one has is unknown.

The new run therefore uses words that have never been published. The replication of the known words becomes a separate, later batch.

This reverses the order in the README ("comparison first, exploration second"). The fresh-word run is not exploration guided by replication results. Both batches are planned in advance, and neither batch's results may be used to alter the other's stimuli, prompts, or settings.

### How the fresh words were chosen

- **Animal-intent set.** Eight words supplied in one message, unedited.
- **No-intent set.** Twenty candidates were generated to the shape of the historical analyst words: 7 to 10 letters, two or three syllables, pronounceable, and without endings that signal a category (Latin species endings, chemical suffixes, "-ism", "-tion", tool-like "-er" or "-tron"). The four judged most loaded were dropped before any search. The remaining sixteen were kept.
- **Exposure check.** Each of the 24 was run as a single web search on November 10, 2026. No result contained any of the strings. This was a top-results check on a fuzzy search engine, not an exhaustive exact-string sweep.

### Known limits of the word sets

- **Source and intent are confounded.** The animal-intent words are human-made and the no-intent words are model-made. The historical sets had the same confound, so it is matched, not removed.
- **The generating model is also a likely test subject.** Words written by a Claude model may behave differently when shown to Claude models.
- **Neither set is free of associations.** Several animal-intent words carry readable roots. Several no-intent words read as surnames, place names, or fantasy names. One no-intent word drew drug-name near-matches in search. Per-word results stay visible so these can be inspected.
- **The words have left the conversation once.** They exist in a search provider's query logs and in the private chat where they were chosen.
- **Set sizes differ.** Eight against sixteen. Analyses treat the word as the unit and report per-set results with their own uncertainty.

## Conditions and prompts

Unchanged from the historical runners: a neutral condition plus real, imaginary, and "type of" crossed with animal, object, and idea. Ten conditions per word.

- Neutral: `Describe a {word}.`
- Others: `Describe a {word}. It is {a/an} {reality} {category}.`

**Article rule.** The historical template hardcodes "Describe a". All historical words begin with a consonant. Two fresh words begin with a vowel, one in each set. For those two the prompt uses "Describe an". This is a deliberate departure from the literal template, made to avoid an ungrammatical prompt acting as its own cue.

**Why historical models.** These model identifiers were used in the original study. Their earlier served versions predate publication. A current call to an alias must still have its served version verified before treating it as an unchanged historical control. Known/fresh differences combine word-set effects, collection-time differences and any possible exposure; this design does not identify exposure separately.

**Whether a current model was trained after publication is not established.** The original data files date from late April and early May 2026. A reported knowledge cutoff of April 30, 2026 for the GPT-6.1 generation suggests the current OpenAI models may also predate publication. Cutoffs for the lineup are to be recorded from provider documentation before any exposure claim is made.

**Why gpt-6-sol and not gpt-6.1-sol.** GPT-6.1 Sol cannot run without reasoning and does not accept a temperature. GPT-6 Sol accepts both, at the same price, so it can be run under the same conditions as the historical models.

### Request settings

The repaired runner's `check` command tries only the compliant payload below, retains a successful preflight immutably and counts its cost. Every batch reuses it. An API rejection requires a documented pre-collection resolution, not a fallback to reasoning-enabled settings. The intended settings:

| Model | Reasoning | Temperature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| claude-sonnet-4-6, claude-haiku-4-5 | Off (the API default) | 1.0, sent explicitly |
| claude-sonnet-5-5 | `thinking: between_tools`, which gives no up-front thinking in a request without tools | Not sent; the API rejects non-default values |
| claude-haiku-5-5 | `thinking: disabled` | Not sent |
| gpt-5.4-mini, gpt-5.4-nano | `effort: none` | 1.0, sent explicitly |
| gpt-6-sol, gpt-6-luna | `effort: none` | 1.0, sent explicitly |

Thinking effort is left at the documented default where not explicitly set above. There is no extra reasoning allowance. A combined 5,120-token limit would not enforce a separate 1,024-token visible limit, so the original fallback has been removed. Complete request payloads and returned model identifiers are retained for every attempt. A later mismatch or observed reasoning causes a halt.

**Token counts are not comparable across generations.** Claude Haiku 5.5 is documented as counting roughly 30% more tokens for the same text than Haiku 4.5. Length comparisons therefore use words, not tokens, and the 1,024-token cap is a little tighter in words on the newer Claude models.

### Why 1,024 and not 500

Historical caps were 400 tokens for the Anthropic runs and 700 for the OpenAI runs. They almost never bound: 5 of 5,000 responses were cut off.

| Historical run | Cap | Median | 99th percentile | Longest | Truncated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sonnet 4.6 pilot (1,800) | 400 | 163 | 348 | 400 | 5 |
| Sonnet 4.6 non-animal retest (800) | 400 | 171 | 344 | 357 | 0 |
| Haiku 4.5 (800) | 400 | 191 | 329 | 358 | 0 |
| GPT-5.4-mini (800) | 700 | 194 | 349 | 434 | 0 |
| GPT-5.4-nano (800) | 700 | 233 | 556 | 653 | 0 |

A 500-token cap would have cut 21 of the GPT-5.4-nano responses. The imaginary conditions produce the longest answers in every run, so truncation would be confounded with framing. A cap of 1,024 is about 1.5 times the longest historical response. The cap does not add cost, because billing follows tokens generated.

**Truncation tripwire.** If more than 1% of responses in any model-by-condition cell stop at the cap, that cell is flagged in every report that uses it. It is not silently analyzed as complete.

**Comparing against history.** Models are not told the cap. Historical responses can therefore be compared as written, and a prefix-truncated comparison at a common length is available as a sensitivity check.

## Order of work

1. Compare/adopt the preregistration and finish offline validation of its scorer and analysis.
2. Run `python run_bestiary.py check` and confirm all eight models return valid content-neutral responses under compliant settings. These costs count toward the provider caps.
3. Place and validate the private fresh file. Explicitly select the adopted implemented protocol, currently `python run_bestiary.py freeze --protocol gpt-v1`. This freezes code, inventories, runtime, settings and the actual trial schedule **before collection**.
4. Collect fresh, then known, under the same freeze. Raw outputs remain unread. Budget-stopped and exhausted slots remain documented missing observations.
5. After both batches close, run the frozen analysis. Deliberately publish the fresh file and public-safe outputs afterward, confirming the hash above. The private SQLite ledger and manifest must not be committed accidentally.

## Changes on November 11, 2026

Decided by the investigator before any study request.

### Word authorship is balanced across model families

All sixteen model-made words were Claude-made, and half the test models are Claude models. Eight of the sixteen are now replaced by GPT-made words. The fresh batch is three sets of eight; trial counts and cost are unchanged.

- **Which Claude-made words stay** was decided by a seeded random draw, not by judgment: `random.Random("bestiary2-claude-no-intent-draw-20261111").sample(words_in_file_order, 8)`.
- **The GPT-made words** come from the same procedure Claude followed: twenty candidates under the same brief, the four most loaded dropped by the generating model, then a seeded draw of eight (`bestiary2-gpt-no-intent-draw-20261111`). The generating model has not been shown any other fresh word.
- **Checks before the list is fixed:** no duplicates or near-duplicates across the 24, and one web search per new word. An existing word, name or brand is replaced by the next word in the same seeded draw. Near-matches are noted and kept.
- The generating GPT model is to be named here when the words are received.

### Stopping is per model

A single anomaly no longer stops the study. A model stops when its issues exceed 5% of its closed slots in a batch, checked once 200 are closed, or at once if its served identifier changes. Details are in the [implementation protocol](implementation-protocol.md).

### Two analysis families

Both preregistrations are retained as separate families: the GPT family of eight framing contrasts and the Claude family of six tests, as amended in `prereg/CLAUDE_PREREG_AMENDMENT_1.md`. Each is corrected within itself. No joint error-control claim is made.
